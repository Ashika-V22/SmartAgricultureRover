# Demetrios Smart Agriculture Rover — Complete Wiring Guide
### Arduino Mega 2560 R3 Edition

This guide uses **only** the real pin numbers printed on the Mega 2560 board. No solar panel. No grass-cutter. Every component you listed is used, and nothing extra is invented.

---

## 0. Why the Mega (and two rules you must never break)

The Mega has way more pins than an Uno, so we can give every sensor/module its own dedicated pin — no multiplexing tricks needed.

**Rule 1 — Never power motors, the pump, or the big MG996R servo from the Arduino's 5V pin.** The Arduino's onboard regulator can only supply ~500mA–1A total. Motors and pumps pull amps. You will fry the board.

**Rule 2 — Everything must share one common ground.** Battery, both L298N boards, the buck converter, the Arduino, every sensor, both servos, the relay, and the ESP32-CAM must all have their GND wires tied to the same ground network. Without this, signals won't read correctly even if power is technically present.

## MEGA PIN MAP (memorize this before you start)

| Pin range | What it's for |
|---|---|
| PWM pins | 2–13, 44–46 |
| Serial0 (0/1) | USB programming — never wire anything else here |
| Serial1 | TX1 = pin 18, RX1 = pin 19 |
| Serial2 | TX2 = pin 16, RX2 = pin 17 |
| Serial3 | TX3 = pin 14, RX3 = pin 15 (kept free/unused in this build) |
| A0–A15 | Analog inputs |

## ENA/ENB and 5V-EN jumper situation (read before touching the L298N boards)

Each L298N board actually has **two different jumpers** — don't confuse them:

1. **The small "5V-EN" jumper near the 12V terminal.** This connects an onboard 5V regulator to the board's 5V-out pin. Since your battery is 12V and you already have a separate buck converter, **remove this jumper on both boards.** Instead, feed the L298N's 5V pin from the Mega's 5V pin (or the buck 5V rail) — this only powers the driver chip's *logic*, which draws a tiny amount of current, so the Mega can handle it fine.
2. **The ENA and ENB jumper caps** (little black jumper blocks sitting directly on the ENA/ENB pins). If left ON, the motor always runs at full speed with no PWM control. Since you want speed control, **remove both ENA and ENB jumper caps on both boards** and wire ENA/ENB to the Mega PWM pins listed above.

## STAGE 1: Arduino + L298N #1 + two motors (right side)

**Components needed:** Mega 2560, L298N #1, Motor 1, Motor 3, a few jumper wires, USB cable for testing (no battery yet).

**Wiring:**
- Mega D2 → L298N #1 ENA
- Mega D22 → L298N #1 IN1
- Mega D23 → L298N #1 IN2
- Mega D24 → L298N #1 IN3
- Mega D25 → L298N #1 IN4
- Mega D3 → L298N #1 ENB
- Motor 1 red/black → OUT1/OUT2
- Motor 3 red/black → OUT3/OUT4
- L298N #1 GND → Mega GND (this is your first ground link)
- Remove ENA/ENB jumper caps and the 5V-EN jumper as described above

**Do NOT connect yet:** the 12V battery. At this stage the motors won't spin at full torque — we're only checking direction logic — so power the L298N logic side only, temporarily, from the Mega 5V pin.

**Test:** Upload a simple sketch that sets IN1=HIGH, IN2=LOW, IN3=HIGH, IN4=LOW, and ENA/ENB=200 (PWM). Motor 1 and Motor 3 should attempt to spin the same direction (they'll be weak/slow without full 12V — that's expected and fine for this test). Reverse the IN pins and confirm both reverse too. If a motor doesn't respond, check its OUT1/OUT2 wiring before moving on.

## STAGE 2: L298N #2 + two motors (left side)

**Components needed:** L298N #2, Motor 2, Motor 4.

**Wiring:**
- Mega D4 → L298N #2 ENA
- Mega D26 → L298N #2 IN1
- Mega D27 → L298N #2 IN2
- Mega D28 → L298N #2 IN3
- Mega D29 → L298N #2 IN4
- Mega D5 → L298N #2 ENB
- Motor 2 red/black → OUT1/OUT2
- Motor 4 red/black → OUT3/OUT4
- L298N #2 GND → same common ground bus as L298N #1
- Remove its ENA/ENB caps and 5V-EN jumper too

**Do NOT connect:** 12V battery yet.

**Test:** Same logic test as Stage 1, but now for Motor 2/Motor 4. Once both stages pass individually, run all four together and confirm forward = all four spin the "forward" direction, and that a left/right turn command spins right-side and left-side motors in opposite directions.

## STAGE 3: Power distribution

**Components needed:** 12V 7Ah battery, LM2596 buck converter, long breadboard, thick-gauge wire for motor power, a power switch (recommended, even if not in your list — highly advisable to add one, otherwise skip if you don't have one).

**Wiring (this is the most important stage — go slow):**
- Battery + → splits three ways using solid wire/terminal block (NOT the breadboard):
  1. → L298N #1 "12V/VS" terminal
  2. → L298N #2 "12V/VS" terminal
  3. → LM2596 buck converter "IN+"
  4. → Relay module's common (COM) terminal, for the pump circuit (details in Stage 8)
- Battery − → common ground bus (direct wire, not breadboard) → also to LM2596 "IN−"
- LM2596 OUT+ : **before connecting anything**, power the buck converter alone and use a multimeter to adjust its trimpot until output reads exactly **5.0V**. This is critical — factory default is often not 5V.
- LM2596 OUT+ → long breadboard's red power rail
- LM2596 OUT− → long breadboard's blue/black ground rail, and this rail also ties into the common ground bus
- Mega 5V pin → long breadboard red rail (this now replaces your USB power once battery is connected)
- Mega GND → long breadboard black rail

**Do NOT put through the breadboard:** the battery-to-L298N 12V wires, and the battery-to-pump wire. These carry high current and belong on solid wire/terminal blocks only. The breadboard is strictly for the low-current 5V logic rail.

**Test:** With everything grounded together, power the whole rig from the battery only (unplug USB). Repeat the Stage 1/2 motor test — now the motors should spin with full torque since they're getting real 12V.

## STAGE 4: Sensors (small breadboard)

**Components needed:** DHT11, soil moisture sensor, HC-SR04, small breadboard, 10kΩ pull-up resistor (only needed if your DHT11 module doesn't already have one built in — most 3-pin breakout boards do).

**Wiring (all through the small breadboard for shared 5V/GND access):**
- DHT11: VCC → 5V rail, GND → ground rail, Data → Mega D12
- Soil moisture sensor: VCC → 5V rail, GND → ground rail, AOUT → Mega A0
- HC-SR04: VCC → 5V rail, GND → ground rail, Trig → Mega D10, Echo → Mega D11

**Do NOT connect:** soil sensor's digital output pin (DOUT) if it has one — you only need the analog pin (AOUT) for real moisture readings.

**Test:** Simple sketch printing DHT11 temp/humidity, soil moisture raw value (dry air ≈ high number, wet soil ≈ low number, or vice versa depending on your exact module — check by touching probes to wet soil), and HC-SR04 distance in cm. Wave your hand in front of the ultrasonic sensor and confirm the distance value changes.

## STAGE 5: HC-05 Bluetooth

**Components needed:** HC-05 module, two resistors for a voltage divider (recommended: 1kΩ and 2kΩ).

**Why the divider:** The Mega's TX pin outputs 5V logic. The HC-05's RX pin is only rated up to about 3.6V. Feeding it 5V directly can damage it over time.

**Wiring:**
- HC-05 VCC → 5V rail, GND → ground rail
- HC-05 TX → Mega RX2 (pin 17) — direct connection is fine, since 3.3V is high enough for the Mega to read as logic HIGH
- Mega TX2 (pin 16) → 1kΩ resistor → HC-05 RX, **and** a 2kΩ resistor from that same junction point down to GND. This divides the 5V signal down to about 3.3V before it reaches the HC-05.

**Do NOT connect:** HC-05 RX directly to Mega TX2 without the divider.

**Test:** Pair the HC-05 with your phone's Bluetooth, open a serial Bluetooth terminal app, and send a test character. Confirm your Mega sketch receives it on Serial2. Then confirm the reverse — Mega prints something and it shows up in the phone app.

---

## STAGE 6: GPS (NEO-6M)

**Components needed:** NEO-6M GPS module.

**Wiring:**
- GPS VCC → 5V rail, GND → ground rail
- GPS TX → Mega RX1 (pin 19)
- GPS RX → Mega TX1 (pin 18)

Most NEO-6M breakout boards include their own voltage regulation and are generally tolerant of direct connection to Mega's 5V-logic TX line, but if you want to be extra safe, use the same resistor-divider trick as Stage 5 on this line too.

**Test:** Use a simple TinyGPS++ style sketch reading Serial1. Take the rover outdoors (GPS needs open sky — it won't get a fix indoors) and confirm latitude/longitude values start printing after a minute or two of acquiring satellites.

---

## STAGE 7: Servos (plough + seed sowing)

**Components needed:** SG90, MG996R.

**Wiring:**
- SG90 signal (usually orange wire) → Mega D6
- MG996R signal (usually orange/yellow wire) → Mega D7
- **Both servos' power wire (red)** → buck converter 5V rail (or a second dedicated buck converter, ideally rated for at least 2A if using both together, since the MG996R alone can spike to 1–2.5A when under load)
- Both servos' ground wire (brown/black) → common ground bus

**Do NOT power servos from the Mega 5V pin** — this is the #1 cause of random resets/browning-out on rover builds.

**Test:** Sweep each servo through its range individually with the Servo library. Watch that the Mega doesn't reset when the MG996R moves under load — if it does, your 5V rail needs more current headroom (bigger/second buck converter).

---

## STAGE 8: Relay + water pump

**Components needed:** 2-channel relay module, 12V water pump.

**Wiring:**
- Relay VCC → 5V rail, GND → ground rail
- Relay IN1 → Mega D8 (this channel controls the pump)
- Relay IN2 → Mega D9 (spare, unused for now)
- Battery + → relay's COM terminal (channel 1)
- Relay's NO (Normally Open) terminal → pump's + wire
- Pump's − wire → common ground bus (back to battery −)

This means the pump's 12V power path runs: **battery → relay contact → pump → ground**, and the relay only carries a small control signal from the Mega — the Mega never touches the pump's high-current path directly.

**Do NOT connect:** the pump directly to any Arduino pin, and do not run the pump's 12V wires through either breadboard.

**Test:** Toggle D8 HIGH/LOW in a sketch and listen for the relay's audible click each time, then confirm the pump turns on/off accordingly with the battery connected.

---

## STAGE 9: ESP32-CAM

**Components needed:** ESP32-CAM module (with either a USB-TTL programmer for flashing it once, or an existing FTDI adapter — used only during setup, not part of the permanent rover wiring).

**Important architecture point:** The ESP32-CAM should be thought of as its **own independent device**, not a Mega peripheral. It has its own WiFi radio and can host its own video stream directly to your phone app — it does **not** need a data connection to the Mega at all for live camera. Trying to send video frames through the Mega's serial pins would be far too slow anyway.

**Wiring (power only):**
- ESP32-CAM 5V pin → buck converter 5V rail (give it a dedicated feed if possible — the camera draws current spikes when capturing/transmitting, and a shared rail with servos can cause brownouts)
- ESP32-CAM GND → common ground bus

**Do NOT wire any ESP32-CAM GPIO to the Mega for the camera feed.** If later you want the Mega to trigger a photo capture, that's a separate, optional addition using Serial3 (pins 14/15) — not required for the current feature set.

**Test:** Flash the standard CameraWebServer example, connect it to your home/hotspot WiFi (or run it as its own access point), and confirm you can view the live stream in a browser or your app before mounting it on the rover.

---

## STAGE 10: Complete integration testing

1. Re-check every ground connection with a multimeter continuity test — battery−, both L298N GNDs, buck converter GND, Mega GND, both breadboard ground rails, both servo grounds, relay GND, ESP32-CAM GND should all beep as connected.
2. Power everything from the battery (no USB).
3. Test movement (forward/backward/left/right/stop) via Bluetooth commands from your app.
4. While driving, confirm sensor readings (soil, temp/humidity, ultrasonic) still update correctly — motor electrical noise can sometimes interfere with sensor readings if grounding is poor, so this is a good stress test.
5. Trigger the plough servo, then the seed-sowing servo, individually while the rover is stationary.
6. Trigger the pump via relay.
7. Open the ESP32-CAM stream on your phone while the rover drives around.
8. Finally, confirm GPS coordinates update while the rover moves outdoors.

If everything in step 3–8 works simultaneously without resets or erratic behavior, your wiring is complete and stable.
